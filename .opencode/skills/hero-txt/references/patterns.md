# What the library shows

Observations from 124 hero sections across 20 categories, captured from live product sites (the count at the time of writing). Current versions are mostly from August–October 2026; earlier versions reach back to 2024. This is a record of what companies shipped, not a leaderboard — newer is not automatically better.

## The shape of the data

- Median headline: **6 words**. 90% are 9 words or shorter; the longest is 13.
- Median subheadline: **14 words**. 90% are 28 or shorter.
- **51** headlines mention AI and **35** mention agents. AI is table stakes; agent is the current wedge.
- Only **2** headlines are questions. The declarative sentence is the default for a reason: it makes a claim.
- **47** products have an earlier version on file. Those pairs are the most useful part of the library, because they show what a company stopped saying.

## Seven moves that repeat

### 1. Sell the job, not the category

The strongest lines name something the buyer is already trying to do. The category label stays in the subheadline, if it appears at all.

- Stripe — **Financial infrastructure to grow your revenue.**
- Resend — **Email for developers**
- Pinecone — **Give agents knowledge**
- AgentMail — **Email Inboxes for AI Agents**
- Browser Use — **Agents that use the browser.**

A category label tells the buyer where to file you. A job tells them why to care.

### 2. Put a number or a named mechanism in the line

Specificity is the cheapest form of credibility. A number, a named stack, or a concrete unit beats an adjective.

- Apify — **65,824 tools for your AI**
- DoiT — **Attribute Every AI Token to the Customer, Feature, and Agent Behind It**
- Akeyless — *Securing 220B+ machine interactions…*
- Modal — *sub-second cold starts, instant autoscaling…*
- Baseten — *Powered by the Baseten Inference Stack.*

The number has to be true and relevant. An impressive number about the wrong thing is still noise.

### 3. Name the enemy or the stakes

This is the clearest shift in the recent captures: from describing the product to naming the tension the buyer already feels.

- Coder — **Your developers have guardrails. Your AI doesn't.**
- Cortex — **Code is no longer the bottleneck. Everything else is.**
- CircleCI — **Validate everything. Assume nothing.**
- Octopus Deploy — **AI is accelerating development. Can your governance keep up?**
- Coralogix — **You can't observe the future with yesterday's architecture**

The enemy can be a practice ("yesterday's architecture"), a default ("assume nothing"), or a gap ("your AI doesn't"). It cannot be a competitor you are not willing to name.

### 4. Lead with a verb

Build, ship, run, give, turn, stop, validate. Verbs make a claim testable; noun phrases float.

- Railway — **Ship software peacefully**
- Daytona — **Run AI Code.**
- Pinecone — **Give agents knowledge**
- Reducto — **Turn documents into data.**

The verb does not have to be imperative. "Inference is everything" works because "is" makes a stance.

### 5. Let the headline make the bet; let the subheadline pay it

The best pairs divide labor. The headline states the wager; the subheadline supplies the mechanism, scope, or proof.

- Vercel — **Agentic Infrastructure** / *For coding agents · To ship apps and agents · Automated by agents.*
- Baseten — **Inference is everything** / *The fastest model runtimes, cross-cloud high availability, and seamless developer workflows.*
- DigitalOcean — **AI-Native Cloud** / *One integrated platform, silicon to agent, with economics that improve as you scale.*
- Neo4j — **The knowledge layer that makes AI trustworthy** / *Lower cost. Reduce risk. Inform decisions.*

If the subheadline could replace the headline without changing the meaning, one of them is redundant.

### 6. Repositioning moves from feature to consequence

The before/after pairs show a consistent direction: drop the category label, keep the consequence, and make the AI claim more specific.

- Akash — *The World's Premier Decentralized Compute Marketplace* → **The Open Cloud for AI's Next Frontier**
- Akeyless — *Vaultless Secrets Management* → **Runtime Identity Security at Agentic Scale**
- Cortex — *Unblock Engineers. Unlock Productivity.* → **Code is no longer the bottleneck. Everything else is.**
- Docker — *Develop faster. Run anywhere.* → **Accelerate agent adoption, safely.**
- CircleCI — *Confidence in every deploy* → **Validate everything. Assume nothing.**
- Harness — *The AI-Native Software Delivery Platform* → **Your harness for the autonomous SDLC**

This is an association, not a proven playbook: the companies that repositioned are also the ones likely to have a new product to sell. Read the pairs as evidence of what stopped landing, not as a formula.

### 7. Compression is a strategy, not a style

The median six-word headline is not an accident of taste. Short lines are easier to remember, easier to localize, and harder to fake. The longest entries in the library are usually the ones doing the most explaining — and explaining is the subheadline's job.

- E2B — **AI Sandboxes for Deep Research Agents** (6 words)
- Cursor — **Cursor is your coding agent for building ambitious software.** (10 words, still one idea)
- Letta — **Letta is an AI research lab in San Francisco building machines that learn.** (13 words; an exception that works because the company is the product)

## What not to copy

- **Corporate announcements.** "Codefresh and Octopus" and "IBM Kubecost" are acquisition/renaming notices. They were live, not strategic.
- **Event pages.** "OpenSearchCon North America" is a conference banner, not evergreen positioning.
- **Internal product names without context.** "Meet LangSmith Engine" assumes the reader already knows what the engine does.
- **All-caps and heavy punctuation.** Two of 124 use it; both are making a deliberate brand choice that most products cannot carry.
- **Slash-separated feature lists.** Vercel can run three fragments in a subheadline because the brand already carries the explanation. Most products cannot.
- **The assumption that newer is better.** Lumigo's current "Intelligent AI-Powered Observability" is more generic than its earlier "Solve complex microservice issues 80% faster." The archive is evidence, not a ranking.

## A short diagnostic

Score 1–5:

| Dimension | Question |
| --------- | -------- |
| Clarity | Can a smart outsider say what it is after one read? |
| Specificity | Is there a number, named mechanism, or concrete job? |
| Credibility | Would a skeptic ask "says who?" — and is there an answer nearby? |
| Differentiation | Could a competitor run the same line unchanged? |
| Compression | Can any word go without losing meaning? |

Anything at 2 or below is a rewrite, not a polish.

## Rewrite moves that show up in the pairs

| Move | Before | After |
| ---- | ------ | ----- |
| Feature → consequence | Vaultless Secrets Management | Runtime Identity Security at Agentic Scale |
| Benefit → enemy | Unblock Engineers. Unlock Productivity. | Code is no longer the bottleneck. Everything else is. |
| Mechanism → job | eBPF-based networking, security, and observability | Connect and Secure Kubernetes. And Beyond. |
| Generic AI → specific agent job | The AI-Native Software Delivery Platform | Your harness for the autonomous SDLC |
| Descriptive → imperative | Confidence in every deploy | Validate everything. Assume nothing. |

Use the move, not the line. The value of the library is the shape of the argument, not the words.
